## 1. Summary

v2.1 builds one **public, sharable profile** for global and national (U.S.) news. Two agenda sources, the New York Times and the Economist, tell the agent what is significant today; the agent then searches freely for the best coverage of each story and produces a daily edition, organized by the Economist's section categories with the top two or three stories in each, of summaries linked to that coverage, readable by anyone with the link. It establishes the records (profile, story, archive, edition, run log, direction), the agent's instructions, the unattended nightly run, and the reading surface. v2.2 adds the owner's private profile and the first weighted view over two profiles.

Choosing a general-news profile first is a good test of the vision for a reason the vision anticipated: it exercises the objective-newsworthiness axis in isolation. The profile-relevance axis gets its real test in v2.2.

## 2. Scope

**In v2.1**
- One profile: `global-us-news`, public, with the Economist's section categories as its beats.
- Two agenda sources: NYT and the Economist, via their public headline feeds. They set the agenda; they do not constrain where coverage comes from.
- Unconstrained search for coverage of each story the agenda surfaces, and for stories the agenda missed.
- The story record, the archive, the edition, the run log, the direction record.
- Agent instructions covering the five roles.
- Unattended nightly run between 2am and 3am Eastern.
- A public, sharable reading surface for editions and the archive.
- A minimal direction channel for the owner.
- Profile evolution by proposal only: the agent proposes, the owner applies.

**Not in v2.1** (from the vision's non-goals, restated for this release)
- A second profile or any view over more than one profile.
- Any private material. Nothing in v2.1 is personal to the owner.
- The browser extension. Direction is owner-only and goes through the minimal channel.
- Full article text from behind any paywall. Summaries are written from publicly accessible coverage.
- Newsletter ingestion, developments, PDF, evals.

## 3. Sources

### 3.1 What a source is

A source **surfaces possible stories**. It does not define where the coverage comes from. The vision puts it as "search is the primary discovery mechanism and feeds are pointers," and this PRD keeps that distinction with two terms:

- An **agenda source** is a publication whose editorial judgment the profile trusts to say what is significant today. The agent reads its headline feeds as a list of candidate stories. For v2.1 the agenda sources are the New York Times and the Economist.
- **Coverage** is whatever the agent finds, by search, that best reports a story the agenda surfaced. Coverage can come from anywhere: a wire service, a public broadcaster, a trade publication, a primary document, or the agenda source itself when its article is the best available. The agent chooses, and the story record says what it chose and why.

Agenda sources inform what the agent looks for. They never constrain what it links to or what it reads to write the summary.

### 3.2 The decision, and the pushback

The owner's bias is two agenda sources, NYT and the Economist. This PRD adopts that. Two points are recorded.

**The agenda is narrow by design, and that is the point to measure.** Two desks with a similar outlook decide what counts as significant. Coverage is unconstrained, so the edition is not limited to what those desks wrote, but it is limited to what they chose to cover. For a public profile claiming "U.S. news," a wire service as a third agenda source would widen what gets surfaced. The recommendation is to launch with two, let discovery search (3.4) catch what they miss, and after two weeks compare the archive against what the discovery searches turned up. If discovery keeps finding significant stories the agenda sources skipped, add a wire service as the first agenda increment. Agenda sources are a profile attribute, so that is a profile edit, not a code change.

**Paywalls are a coverage question, not an agenda question.** The NYT and Economist feeds need no login, so they serve as agenda freely. Their articles are paywalled, so they are often not the best coverage link for a public reader. The agent prefers accessible coverage when it is of comparable quality and marks any link that leads to a subscriber site. A summary is written from the coverage the agent actually read, so it does not depend on text the agent cannot reach.

### 3.4 Search

The agent searches in two ways, neither constrained to the agenda sources.

**Coverage search.** For each candidate the agenda surfaced, find the best coverage: the report that is most complete, most authoritative, and most accessible, in that order of tie-breaking. Read it. Write the summary from it. Record every piece of coverage consulted. Where a primary document exists (a ruling, a filing, a statement), link it alongside the report.

**Discovery search.** For each category in the profile, search for the day's significant news independent of the agenda. A story found this way is a candidate like any other and is marked in the record as discovered rather than agenda-surfaced. The ratio of discovered to agenda-surfaced stories, over time, is the evidence for whether the agenda is too narrow.

## 4. The profile

The profile is a file the agent reads every run and the owner edits. Its content for v2.1:

```yaml
id: global-us-news
name: Global and U.S. News
visibility: public
description: >
  The day's significant news, worldwide and in the United States, as surfaced by the
  New York Times and the Economist, organized by the Economist's sections, with the
  top two or three stories in each, summarized and linked.
cadence: daily, run between 06:00 and 07:00 UTC
edition_size: 2 to 3 stories per category; a category with nothing significant is omitted

categories:                 # the "beats" of this profile: the Economist's sections, verbatim
  - { id: leaders,                 name: Leaders,                 weight: 1.0 }
  - { id: briefing,                name: Briefing,                weight: 1.0 }
  - { id: united-states,           name: United States,           weight: 1.0 }
  - { id: international,           name: International,           weight: 1.0 }
  - { id: finance-and-economics,   name: Finance and economics,   weight: 1.0 }
  - { id: business,                name: Business,                weight: 1.0 }
  - { id: europe,                  name: Europe,                  weight: 1.0 }
  - { id: asia,                    name: Asia,                    weight: 1.0 }
  - { id: china,                   name: China,                   weight: 1.0 }
  - { id: middle-east-and-africa,  name: Middle East and Africa,  weight: 1.0 }
  - { id: the-americas,            name: The Americas,            weight: 1.0 }
  - { id: science-and-technology,  name: Science and technology,  weight: 1.0 }
# Weights are all 1.0 at launch. They exist so that personal signals (SFL) and a later
# private view can reweight them without changing the profile's shape.
# "The world this week" is read as agenda only; it is a digest, not a category.
# Every candidate, from either agenda source or from discovery, is assigned to exactly one
# category by the agent. NYT feeds map naturally: World to the regional categories,
# U.S. and Politics to United States, Business to Business or Finance and economics.

entities: []                 # none for a general profile; v2.2 will populate

deprioritize:
  - celebrity and entertainment news
  - sports, except an event of national or international consequence
  - individual crime stories without systemic significance
  - opinion and commentary, unless the edition is explicitly surfacing a leader
  - incremental product announcements
  - live-blog and "what we know" rolling formats

agenda_sources:              # surface candidates; never constrain coverage
  - publisher: New York Times
    feeds: [ ...section 3.3... ]
    window_hours: 48
  - publisher: The Economist
    feeds: [ ...section 3.3... ]
    window_hours: 48

coverage:
  search: unconstrained      # best coverage from anywhere
  prefer: [complete, authoritative, accessible]
  mark_subscriber_links: true
  discovery_per_category: true   # also search each category independent of the agenda

editorial:
  two_axes: true            # newsworthiness and category fit scored separately
  summary_length: 2 to 4 sentences
  summary_rule: original prose, written from coverage the agent read; no verbatim passages beyond a short attributed quote
  mark_thin_summaries: true # when no readable coverage was found and the summary rests on headline and description

evolution:
  mode: propose             # agent proposes profile edits in the edition; owner applies
```

## 5. Records

Five records. Exact file formats are set in section 8; this section defines content.

**Story**

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| id | date plus slug, unique across the archive |
| headline | the agent's headline, which may differ from the source's |
| surfaced_by | the agenda source and feed that surfaced it, or `discovery` with the category searched |
| url | the best coverage link the agent chose |
| coverage | every piece of coverage consulted: publisher, link, whether read in full, whether behind a paywall |
| published | publication timestamp of the linked coverage |
| surfaced | the run that kept it |
| profile | `global-us-news` |
| category | exactly one category id |
| newsworthiness | 0 to 1, judged on coverage breadth, magnitude, consequence, novelty |
| relevance | 0 to 1, fit to its category; at launch this mostly confirms the assignment, and it is where personal signals will act later |
| summary | two to four sentences, original prose |
| why_it_matters | one or two sentences, optional for a general profile when the summary already says it |
| basis | which items in `coverage` the summary was written from; `headline-only` when nothing readable was found |
| directed | true when the story came from owner direction |
| related | ids of earlier archive stories about the same matter, if any, so a later development layer can be built |

**Archive.** Every story ever kept, append-only, one file per day. The agent reads the last 14 days before selecting so a story is not repeated. A story is a repeat when it reports the same event as an archived story with no new fact; a genuine new development of an archived matter is a new story with `related` pointing back.

**Edition.** One per day. Content and form in section 6.

**Run log.** One per run: start and end time, feeds fetched and item counts, feeds that failed, candidates considered, stories kept, repeats dropped and why, direction handled, searches performed, estimated cost, and a status of `ok`, `degraded` (some sources failed), or `failed`. A missing run log for a scheduled slot is itself a failure the owner should see.

**Direction.** An append-only inbox the owner writes to and the agent consumes. Each entry: timestamp, action (`research`, `file`, `add-interest`, `freeform`), payload (a link, text, or both), and after handling, the agent's note of what it did and a pointer to the result. v2.1 supports all four actions at a minimum level; the agent reads the inbox at the start of every run and in any interactive session.

## 6. The edition

**Form.** A single page. Masthead with profile name and date. Then two sections.

*Today* is three one-line pointers to the day's most significant stories, each linking down to its full entry. *Sections* follows, one per category in the profile, ordered by the day's significance, each holding the top two or three stories in that category by newsworthiness. A category with nothing significant that day is omitted rather than padded. A story appears in exactly one category. Each story: headline, the coverage it links to with date, summary, and when applicable a small mark for a subscriber-only link, a thin summary, or a directed item. A second line may list further coverage, including the agenda source's own article.

With twelve categories the edition runs roughly twenty to thirty stories. That is wider than the vision's ten-minute target, and deliberately so: the owner's instruction is to start broad and narrow by evidence. The archive and later personal signals are what narrow it.

A closing block, *From the desk*, carries any profile edit the agent proposes (with its reason), any direction it handled, and a one-line run status. This is where the owner sees that the agent is thinking, and where failures surface.

**Example entry**

> **Supreme Court takes up state challenge to federal tariff authority**
> Reuters · Oct 17 · [link]
> The court agreed to hear a challenge by twelve states to the executive branch's use of emergency powers to impose import tariffs, setting up a ruling by June. The case consolidates three lower-court decisions that split on whether the 1977 statute reaches tariffs at all. Markets moved little, but trade lawyers expect the ruling to set the boundary for a generation.
> *Also: SCOTUSblog analysis · the court's order (PDF) · New York Times (subscriber)*

**Length target.** The section structure makes the page scannable even when it is long: a reader goes to the sections they care about. If the day's news is thin in a category, the category is short or absent; the agent does not pad.

## 7. Agent instructions

The agent's instructions are a document in the repository that every run and every interactive session reads first. It is the product; everything else is tooling. Its sections, in the order the agent executes them:

1. **Orient.** Read the profile, the last 14 days of the archive, the direction inbox, and the last run log.
2. **Handle direction.** For each unhandled entry, perform the action, write the result, and mark the entry handled.
3. **Gather.** Fetch every agenda feed within the window. Record counts and failures. Run the per-category discovery searches. The result is a candidate list; nothing has been read yet.
4. **Aggregate.** Collapse items reporting the same event into one candidate with the best link. Assign each candidate to exactly one category. Drop candidates that repeat an archived story with no new fact. Drop candidates matching the deprioritize list unless tightly coupled to a category.
5. **Cover.** For each surviving candidate, search for coverage, choose the best link, and read it. Record everything consulted.
6. **Edit.** Score each candidate on both axes separately. In each category, select the top two or three by newsworthiness; omit a category with nothing significant. Choose the three *Today* pointers across all categories. Write the summary from the coverage read; never reproduce source text beyond a short attributed quote. Write the headline in the agent's own words. Mark subscriber-only links and headline-only summaries.
7. **Reflect.** Note anything that suggests a profile edit: a category that is consistently empty or overflowing, a deprioritized topic that kept earning its way in, a source that failed repeatedly. Write proposals, not changes.
8. **Publish.** Write the stories to the archive, write the edition, write the run log, and make the edition visible.
9. **Report.** If any source failed or the run degraded, say so in the edition's closing block in plain words.

The instructions also carry the editorial standard in prose: what newsworthiness means for this profile, what counts as a repeat, how to treat an Economist leader versus a news report, how to choose between two pieces of coverage, and the rule that a summary must be true to what the agent read and self-sufficient for a reader who opens no link at all.

## 8. System architecture

The vision deferred architecture to this PRD. Three options were considered; one is recommended for v2.1, with the alternatives recorded so a later release can revisit.

### 8.1 Options

**A. Scheduled cloud agent session, repository as the store, static site as the surface.** A Claude Code routine runs on a cron in Anthropic's cloud, checks out a GitHub repository, runs the instructions with web search, fetch, file, and git tools, commits the archive, edition, and run log, and pushes. GitHub Pages renders the editions directory as a public site. Direction is a file in the repository the owner edits from any device. No servers, no database, no deployment. Everything the agent knows is a file, which matches the vision's "agent-native, owner-inspectable" requirement directly. Build cost fits the three-to-four-hour budget.

**B. A hosted service built on the Agent SDK.** A small application on a server or serverless platform runs the agent on a schedule, stores records in a database, and serves editions from a web application. Direction arrives through an HTTP endpoint, which is what a browser extension will eventually want. More flexible, more work: a day or two before the first edition, plus hosting to operate.

**C. Local scheduled run.** Claude Code on the owner's machine under cron or launchd. Free and simple, and it is what v1 did, including the part where it stopped silently.

### 8.2 Recommendation

**Option A for v2.1.** It is hosted, it needs no infrastructure, it fits the budget, and the repository-as-store carries the archive, the profile history, and the run logs as plain diffs. When the browser extension arrives, direction can move to an endpoint without changing anything else; when a second profile and a view arrive in v2.2, they are directories and a weights file. Option B is the right shape if v2 becomes a multi-reader product; the records defined in section 5 transfer to it unchanged.

Two things to verify before building, since they are assumptions about the cloud environment rather than facts established in this session: that the routine's environment permits outbound network access to the feed hosts and to search, and that the routine can push to the repository. Both are checked in the first hour of the build plan.

### 8.3 Repository layout under option A

```
personalpaper/                      # new public repository (see 8.4)
  CLAUDE.md                         # points every session at the agent instructions
  AGENT.md                          # the agent instructions (section 7)
  profiles/
    global-us-news/
      profile.yaml                  # section 4
      archive/
        2026-10-18.md               # one file per day, stories kept
      editions/
        2026-10-18.md               # one file per day
      runs/
        2026-10-18T06.md            # run log
  direction/
    inbox.md                        # owner appends; agent handles and annotates
  site/                             # GitHub Pages source; editions copied or linked here
  tools/                            # only deterministic helpers: feed fetch, dedupe key
```

The profile directory is self-contained so that v2.2 adds `profiles/<private>/` beside it and a `views/` directory with a weights file, with no change to v2.1's files.

### 8.4 A new repository, not the v1 repository

The v1 repository's history contains the owner's personal interest graph, including health details. Making that repository public would publish them. v2.1 therefore starts a **new public repository**, named `personal-paper`, and the v1 repository stays local and private. The v1 working directory is renamed `personal-paper-v1` so the canonical name goes to the live project. v1 is referenced from the new repository's readme as prior work; nothing is copied except what this PRD specifies. The working-drive structure (`/project-setup`) is rendered into the new repository, which is empty, so the renderer's empty-target check passes without force.

### 8.5 Models, by agent function

The five roles do not need the same model, and under option A they do not have to share one. A Claude Code session runs one model as the orchestrator, and it can delegate bounded tasks to subagents on a different model. The instructions assign work accordingly.

| Function | Work | Model | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scraper: feed fetch | Fetch 18 feeds, parse items, filter by window | No model; a deterministic tool in `tools/` | Pure I/O; a model adds cost and nondeterminism |
| Scraper: search and page reading | Discovery searches, coverage searches, reading articles | Sonnet-class subagents, one per candidate, in parallel | Comprehension and extraction; parallelism keeps the orchestrator's context small |
| Aggregator | Collapse duplicates, check the archive for repeats | Sonnet-class, in the orchestrator or a subagent | Reading comprehension against a known list |
| Editor | Score both axes, select, order, write summaries and headlines, reflect | Opus-class, the orchestrator | Judgment is the product; v1 reached the same split and it held |
| Operator | Commit, push, run log, status | Whatever the orchestrator is | No judgment required |
| Interface | Handle direction | The orchestrator | Direction needs the editor's judgment |

**Recommendation for v2.1.** Orchestrator on the Opus-class model, coverage subagents on the Sonnet-class model, feed fetching as a tool. The orchestrator's context stays small because subagents do the reading, so the Opus-class cost is paid mainly on the editorial step. Cost per run is recorded in every run log; if it is out of line, the first lever is the Sonnet-class model as orchestrator, compared on two weeks of editions.

## 9. Public and sharable

- The repository is public. The profile, archive, editions, run logs, and agent instructions are all readable by anyone.
- The edition is served at a public URL per day, with an index. Sharing an edition is sharing a link.
- The direction inbox is public to read but written only by the owner (repository write access). The agent treats only entries in that file as direction; nothing in a story's content is ever treated as an instruction.
- Summaries are original prose with attribution and a link to the coverage they were written from. The agent never reproduces more than a short attributed quote, and marks any link that leads to a subscriber site.
- **License: MIT**, decided 2026-10-17. Adoption is the first hurdle and MIT is the lowest-friction license for it. Tightening later is possible for the owner's own code and for future versions, but not retroactively: code already released under MIT stays MIT for anyone who has it, and relicensing contributions from other people needs their consent, so a contributor agreement should be adopted before accepting outside contributions if a later move to copyleft is a live option. Editions and the archive carry the same license.

## 10. Build plan

Four blocks, about an hour each. Each ends in something checkable.

**Block 1: Repository and structure.** Create the public repository. Render the working-drive structure. Write `profile.yaml` from section 4, the empty archive, editions, and runs directories, the direction inbox, and `CLAUDE.md`. Verify the cloud environment's network access and push rights by creating a one-time routine that fetches one feed and pushes a file. *Check: the one-time run's commit appears in the repository.*

**Block 2: Agent instructions and first manual edition.** Write `AGENT.md` from section 7. Run the instructions in an interactive session against today's feeds. Read the edition. Correct the instructions where the edition is wrong, thin, or repetitive. Re-run. *Check: an edition the owner would read tomorrow.*

**Block 3: Unattended run.** Create the nightly routine with the instructions as its prompt and the repository as its source, on a cron between 06:00 and 07:00 UTC. Trigger it once manually. *Check: a second edition, archive file, and run log appear from the routine, and the archive prevented repeats of the first edition's stories.*

**Block 4: Reading surface.** Enable GitHub Pages on the editions directory or a `site/` directory with a one-page template. Open it on the iPad. *Check: the edition reads cleanly on the iPad at a public URL.*

If block 4 does not fit, the edition is still readable as a rendered markdown file at its repository URL, which is public, and the surface becomes the first task of the next session.

## 11. Success criteria, first two weeks

- Fourteen consecutive editions from the routine with no manual intervention. A degraded run counts; a missing run does not.
- Zero repeated stories across editions, checked by reading the archive.
- Every summary true to the coverage it links, checked by the owner spot-reading five per week against the linked article.
- The share of stories with a subscriber-only primary link, and the share of discovered versus agenda-surfaced stories, both recorded so the agenda decision can be revisited with evidence.
- At least one profile proposal from the agent that the owner accepts.
- At least one direction entry handled end to end.
- The owner reads the edition on the iPad most mornings and prefers it to opening the NYT app for the same ten minutes.
- Cost per edition recorded in every run log and reviewed at the end of the period.

## 12. Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Cloud environment blocks feed hosts or search | Medium | Verified in block 1; fallback is option C for the first week while the environment is configured |
| No readable coverage found for a story | Low | Summary marked headline-only; owner spot-reads; frequency tracked in run logs |
| Two-desk agenda too narrow for "U.S. news" | Medium | Discovery search runs every night; the discovered-to-agenda ratio over two weeks decides whether a wire service joins the agenda |
| Agent links the agenda source's paywalled article when accessible coverage existed | Medium | Coverage preference order in the profile; subscriber links marked; owner spot-checks |
| Feed format or URL changes | Low | Run log records failures; degraded runs surface in the edition |
| Routine fires but produces nothing | Low | A missing run log for a slot is the signal; the owner checks the index until notifications exist |
| Publisher objects to summaries | Low | Original prose, short length, attribution, link to the coverage read; standard aggregator practice |
| Cost higher than expected from coverage and discovery searches across twelve categories | Medium | Recorded per run; cap searches per story and per category in the instructions if needed |

**On decision 4, the two modes.** In *propose* mode the agent writes each suggested change to the profile in the edition's closing block and to a proposals file, with the evidence (a category empty for five days, a deprioritized topic that earned its way in three times, a feed that failed all week). The owner applies by editing `profile.yaml` or by telling the agent to in a session. The profile stays stable and owner-authored, which matters for a public profile other readers may come to rely on; the cost is that nothing evolves unless the owner acts, which is how v1's interest graph went untouched for five weeks. In *apply-and-log* mode the agent edits `profile.yaml` in the run's commit, appends a changelog entry, and says what it changed in the edition. The owner reverts with git if they disagree. Evolution actually happens, and a git-backed store makes it safer than it sounds, because every change is a reviewable diff; the cost is drift, and over-tuning to one day's news. A bounded form sits between them: the agent may move weights within a range and add deprioritize entries on its own, but adding or removing categories or agenda sources is a proposal. The recommendation is propose-only for v2.1 because the profile is public and the first two weeks are for learning what the proposals look like, then bounded apply-and-log for the private profile in v2.2, where drift is cheap and the owner is the only reader.

## 14. Deferred from this review (SFL)

Recorded so the project tracker carries them from day one.

- **Personal signals into the editor.** The owner's intent is that the broad public edition narrows over time through signals the editor agent can ingest: implicit (which stories get opened, which require a surface that can observe clicks) or explicit (thumbs up and down on a story, which require a write path from the edition back to the agent). Either one is the mechanism by which a private view emerges from the public profile, and the category weights exist to receive it. Not in v2.1; the first design question is which signal is cheap enough to collect on a static reading surface.
- **A single-source variant.** The owner considered seeding the public profile from the Economist alone and letting personal signals shape a private view, and set it aside as too much scope for a first release. It stays as a possible direction once signals exist.
- **Adding agenda sources** through profile evolution, with the discovered-to-agenda ratio as the evidence (decision 1).
